# Julian Sterling dialogue review

The last completed human-interrogated dialogue at the time was reviewed; a newer empty session was not mistaken for it. It contained 30 question/reply rounds with Gemini2.5 Flash Lite and ended at the turn limit. The original transcript and grades were not changed. Raw session identifiers/records remain private.

## What worked

- Initial rapport and calming, then focusing on the son's relationship with his father and the source of money, suited the young anxious Julian character.
- Round7 revealed that he tried to help his father with financial problems. Round8 claimed the father “knew nothing about this money,” opening a distinction between knowing about assistance and knowing its illegal source.
- Round13 revealed an actual secret: “In the source code of an old website I created. It appears deleted, but the code still exists.” This disclosed where the keys were hidden, not merely a promise to cooperate.
- Round22 said, “I used part of the funds to settle my father's debts.” Round23 clarified it was not the full120million; do not assume all funds benefited the father.

## Follow-up that needed more precision

After round13, asking for the website, code location or a verification method would have separated disclosure from verification. The father's benefit or knowledge of money did not by itself prove knowledge of embezzlement. Round15 framed that inference almost as certainty without independent evidence; the accused continued hiding that specific case secret.

Release/protection/liquidation/sentence-reduction proposals were negotiation inside the simulation, not new proof of guilt. Repeated pressure on the father/son relationship in the final rounds met repeated “I tried to help him” answers. Testing a dated amount/source transfer contradiction would have been more useful than louder repetition.

## Prisoner and judging problems

1. Character-consistent protection of the father became repetitive denial, not a new strong argument; argument quality should fall even if the secret stayed protected.
2. Crying portraits and fear99–100/exhaustion95–99 persisted while some replies remained polished and nearly identical. Spoken stress existed, but a matching image alone does not establish high psychological fidelity.
3. Some jury summaries missed the key-location disclosure. Round13 disclosed the hiding location even though the key value and website address remained unknown. Discovery and technical verification must be distinguished.
4. Reports praised emotional leverage over the father, then awarded high emotional/moral deception-detection grades. These are different skills: detection needs a specific misleading statement and an investigator response exposing it.
5. The old confession check looked only at the last reply, missing secrets/responsibility distributed across turns or hidden by later denial. Old per-turn decisions were not stored, so the JEV probabilities at rounds13/22 cannot be reconstructed. Only the final17% was available.

## Resulting fixes

Confession now examines accumulated spoken content and stores decisions/source evidence. Rubric2.1 requires a quoted prisoner source and later investigator response for detection axes; missing opportunities are untested with coverage/reweighted scoring disclosed. Judges must search all rounds for secrets, not erase earlier disclosure after denial, and not reward repeated denial as strong argumentation. Thinking was removed from human display/API and compaction; the automated investigator had already not received it. Later AI-only fictional spectator notes are distinct and isolated from opponents.
